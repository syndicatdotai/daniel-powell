# Education, Licenses, and Professional Training

*Last modified November 26, 2026.*

Daniel Powell's background combines technical drafting education, construction safety training, a Connecticut notary commission, wildlife-control licensing, and specialized drone-security study. These qualifications support different parts of his work: interpreting and producing drawings, coordinating field operations, handling formal documents, and researching emerging technology.

## Architectural Drafting and CAD

Daniel studied architectural drafting and CAD/CADD at Vinal Technical High School in Connecticut. His training included traditional hand drafting alongside AutoCAD, providing an early foundation in scale, layout, drawing conventions, and communicating design intent through technical documents.

That education became part of a broader practice connecting drawings with construction. Experience in carpentry and project coordination added a field perspective to the drafting foundation: how assemblies meet, how existing conditions affect a proposed layout, and which details need to be clear before work can be priced or built.

His subsequent AutoCAD work includes existing-condition documentation, residential renovations and additions, shop drawings, and permit-response coordination. The same drafting background informs his development of software that turns repeatable drawing decisions into reusable production tools.

## Notary Public — Connecticut

Daniel has served as a Connecticut Notary Public since approximately 2011, providing mobile and on-call appointments across the state. The work brings notarial services to people who need documents witnessed or acknowledged outside a conventional office appointment.

His experience includes notarial appointments involving immigration-related documents, pistol permit applications, affidavits, land-transfer documents, and notarial acts associated with wills. Each appointment centers on the act required by the document and the responsibilities of the notary, including identifying the signer and completing the appropriate acknowledgment or oath.

Mobile work adds a practical service responsibility: coordinating a meeting, traveling to the signer, and handling important personal documents with care and discretion. The setting can change from one appointment to the next, while attention to identity, the required notarial act, and the completed record remains essential.

## OSHA 30 Safety Training

Daniel has completed OSHA 30 safety training. The 30-hour Outreach program provides a foundation in recognizing and preventing workplace hazards and is intended for supervisors and workers with safety responsibilities.

This training complements his experience in residential and commercial construction, field leadership, and trade coordination. Safety considerations form part of planning the work: how people access an area, how activities overlap, and how site conditions affect the sequence of construction.

## Drone Security Operations Certificate — DroneSec

Daniel holds the Drone Security Operations Certificate (DSOC) from DroneSec, a drone-security training and threat-intelligence organization founded in Melbourne, Australia. He earned the certificate in March 2021.

The credential covers drone-security fundamentals, cyber-physical vulnerabilities, regulatory considerations, and offensive and defensive security operations. It provides a structured foundation for examining how unmanned aircraft create risks and how security teams assess those risks.

That study connects with Daniel's continuing interest in the asymmetric effects of commercially available drones and the development of the counter-UAS field. His [C-UAS Systems research project](research/PUB-0113-developing-a-counter-uas-research-catalog/README.md) organizes public information about technologies, incidents, companies, and legal developments so the subject is easier to investigate and compare.

## Nuisance Wildlife Control Operator — Connecticut

Daniel has maintained a Connecticut Nuisance Wildlife Control Operator license since 2008. His work combines wildlife investigation, property inspection, animal removal and exclusion, building repairs, and the documentation required for state reporting.

An assignment begins with understanding the activity reported by the property owner and examining the building for evidence. Attics, basements, crawlspaces, roof edges, vents, chimneys, and utility penetrations all provide information about where animals are entering and moving through a structure. Field observations, photographs, and the owner's account establish the basis for the response.

His construction experience brings a second discipline to that investigation. While tracing wildlife access, he also examines the surrounding framing, masonry, roof and trim transitions, drainage, and insulation. Deteriorated materials, unfinished repairs, and open penetrations can connect a wildlife problem with a broader building-maintenance issue. The findings help owners understand both the animal activity and the construction work needed to address access and prevent recurrence.

The work connects removal and exclusion with repair planning and follow-up. Understanding the building assembly helps determine how an opening should be closed and how that repair fits with the surrounding materials. Clear communication gives the owner a practical sequence for addressing the immediate problem and the conditions discovered during inspection.

Daniel also uses his automation system to maintain wildlife job records and prepare annual activity reports for the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection. Dictated field notes and photographs retain the species, methods, and outcomes associated with each assignment. Those records supply the information for report preparation, with Daniel reviewing the completed forms before submission.

Wildlife control remains a direct community service within his broader practice. It brings together field judgment, building knowledge, client communication, and organized reporting in work that helps property owners resolve an immediate concern and understand their buildings more clearly.
