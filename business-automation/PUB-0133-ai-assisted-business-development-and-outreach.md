# Using AI to Build a Business Development and Outreach Workflow

Business development involves a sequence of connected tasks: identifying potential customers, understanding their businesses, finding an appropriate contact, preparing relevant communication, and keeping track of what happens afterward.

I used AI to help organize and carry out these business operations for my own business. The effort included prospect research, audience selection, outreach preparation, and maintaining records across different stages of the process.

Alongside that work, I used AI-assisted software development in ChatGPT and Cursor to build custom research and data-processing tools. Those experiments gave me experience collecting public business information, organizing it into structured records, and preparing focused summaries for further use.

Together, these projects explored a practical business question: how can a small operation develop a more systematic approach to finding and communicating with potential customers?

## Starting with a Relevant Business Opportunity

The starting point was deciding which businesses had a reasonable connection to the work I could offer.

My outreach covered several professional audiences, including construction businesses, private investigators, and legal professionals. Each audience had different workflows, terminology, and reasons to consider AI-assisted services or training.

Construction outreach drew on my experience with project documentation, field information, estimates, drawings, and administrative work. Research-oriented outreach involved a different set of applications, including gathering information, organizing source material, and preparing documents.

Those distinctions shaped the research. A company name and an email address were only part of a useful prospect record. The record also needed enough context to explain why the organization belonged in that particular outreach group.

This made relevance a working requirement throughout the process.

## Using AI to Research Potential Customers

AI-assisted research helped gather and organize public information about prospective businesses and professional organizations.

The research considered the type of work an organization performed, its location, its public website, and the connection between its activities and the intended offer. Depending on the audience, an appropriate recipient could be an owner, an operational leader, a person responsible for training, or a published organizational contact.

That context supported a more informed decision about whether to include a prospect.

For construction-related research, I also developed a prospecting framework around smaller and mid-sized businesses whose operations could be relevant to websites, automation, AI, or knowledge-management support.

Public information provided a starting point for that assessment. It helped identify questions worth exploring in a conversation. A website alone could not establish how a company managed its internal work, so observations about potential opportunities remained distinct from confirmed business needs.

## Information Management and Working Records

As the research developed, consistent records became important.

The outreach workflow maintained information about prospective recipients, supporting sources, the applicable audience, and the preparation or communication status. That gave the work continuity across separate research and drafting sessions.

Duplicate checks helped establish whether an organization or recipient had already been considered or contacted. Unresolved matches and uncertain details could be held for review instead of being filled in from assumptions.

The distinction between stages also mattered. A researched prospect, a prepared message, a saved email draft, and a sent message represented different states of the work.

Keeping those states separate made it easier to understand what had actually happened and what still required a decision.

## AI Implementation Through Custom Research Tools

Related experiments explored how custom scripts could support the information-gathering side of this work.

Using ChatGPT and Cursor, I developed Python and JavaScript tools for browser-based collection, website research, data cleanup, comparison, and structured summarization. The tools were developed through separate projects and provided experience with methods relevant to business research.

The software included Playwright for browser automation, BeautifulSoup for HTML processing, Pydantic for structured validation, and OpenAI API calls for language-based processing.

These components performed different jobs. Browser automation interacted with source pages. Processing scripts organized the collected information. Language-model calls prepared summaries from defined inputs. Structured files preserved the results for later operations.

This modular approach to business process automation made it possible to work on a particular part of the process without rebuilding the whole thing. A change to the information being collected could be addressed in the collection tool; a change to the summary format could be addressed in the processing step.

The outreach workflow and the scripting experiments were related pieces of work. They did not depend on a single fully integrated marketing platform.

## Preparing Communication for Different Audiences

Research became useful when it informed a relevant introduction.

I developed distinct outreach approaches for different professional groups. The underlying experience remained mine, while the communication focused on the applications most relevant to the recipient's work.

For construction businesses, that included the relationship between field experience and practical AI workflows. Other professional audiences required a different explanation of the research, documentation, or administrative work being discussed.

The workflow used established message templates for each audience, with defined fields for the information that could change between recipients. That preserved the intended offer and wording while allowing the message to identify the appropriate organization and contact.

This was a useful application of AI as a production assistant. Research, record preparation, and message assembly could be supported by the tools while the business positioning remained deliberate.

## Connecting Preparation with Review and Delivery

The outreach process extended into preparing and reviewing email drafts.

Review covered the recipient, the relevance of the organization, the selected message, and the details carried into the draft. Records also distinguished material prepared locally from drafts saved in the email system.

Sending remained a separate action requiring my direction. That separation allowed research and preparation to progress without treating a newly identified prospect as an automatic instruction to contact someone.

The project included completed outreach as well as further research and drafts. The tracking process preserved those distinctions, supporting later review of correspondence and responses.

This connected the initial prospect search with an organized communication process rather than leaving the work as a collection of disconnected lists and documents.

## Process Improvement in Business Development

My construction background influenced how I approached the workflow.

On a project, progress depends on knowing the scope, identifying the next action, preparing the required information, and understanding who is responsible for a decision. Business development presented a similar organizational problem.

A prospect needed sufficient research before drafting. A draft needed the right audience and wording. A communication needed an accurate record of whether it had been sent. A response became part of the context for the next decision.

AI helped with the preparation and organization surrounding those steps. My responsibility was to define the offer, assess the relevance of the opportunity, review the communication, and decide how to proceed.

The tools supported a business process whose quality depended on those decisions.

## What the Work Demonstrates

This retrospective brings together practical outreach work and the development of supporting research methods.

It demonstrates experience using AI to organize prospect research, distinguish professional audiences, prepare consistent communication, maintain working records, and develop custom tools around recurring information tasks.

The projects were useful as experiments in business operations. They gave me a way to examine how AI-assisted research, scripting, and document preparation could contribute to the work of finding potential customers and opening relevant conversations.

The lasting value was a practical approach to workflow automation: define a process, separate it into manageable stages, and build support around the parts that required repeated effort.
