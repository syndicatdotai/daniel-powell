# Building Haddam Research Group Around Practical AI Research Workflows

## Project Summary

I developed Haddam Research Group around the practical work involved in using AI for research: collecting information, organizing it, checking the sources, and producing something another person can review and use.

The website brings together research workflow consulting, training, AI assisted research support, and private infrastructure. The focus is on helping people build a repeatable process around their actual work.

## Overview

A research assignment can involve public records, documents, websites, correspondence, and notes collected over time. AI can help work through that material, but the quality of the result depends on how the information is handled along the way.

I wanted Haddam Research Group to address that whole process. Where does the material come from? How is it organized? Which statements have supporting evidence? Can someone return to the original source and understand how a conclusion was reached?

The [Haddam Research Group website](https://haddamresearchgroup.com/) describes this approach for legal, investigative, media, and corporate research settings.

## Role

I founded Haddam Research Group and developed its website and service direction. My work on the project has included refining the research workflows, shaping the training offerings, developing supporting content, and maintaining the site.

My background in construction, project management, and software development influences how I approach it. I am interested in workflows that fit the way people actually work and leave behind useful documentation.

## Scope Of Work

The published offering covers three connected areas.

The first is workflow design: helping a person or team define how information moves from intake through collection, extraction, verification, and delivery.

The second is research support. Depending on the assignment, that can mean organizing entity information, reconstructing a timeline, maintaining an evidence log, or preparing a structured research packet.

The third is the infrastructure behind the work, including local knowledge systems, document extraction scripts, retrieval workflows, and tools tailored to a particular process.

Training runs through these areas. The aim is to help people understand and operate the workflow themselves, with review responsibilities established along the way.

## Key Actions

I organized the site around these services and developed the supporting explanations of how they fit together. That included making the distinction between generating an answer and producing research that someone can inspect.

I also developed the website's blog and research content, refined its contact process, and worked on its usability across devices. As the project developed, education and training became a stronger part of the direction.

The training approach includes working around an active research problem and helping an internal team member become comfortable managing the process. That keeps the discussion connected to the documents, questions, and constraints the team already has.

## Important Features

### Source linked research

The workflow treats model output as draft analysis. Statements need to be checked against the underlying material, with citations retained so someone can revisit the evidence.

Archived source copies are part of the methodology described on the site. That matters when a page changes or a research project needs to be revisited later.

### Structured, connected records

Documents often contain useful information scattered across different sections and files. Extracting dates, entities, and relationships into structured records makes that information easier to compare.

Markdown and Obsidian provide a way to keep the resulting notes connected. A timeline can link back to a source record, and an entity note can connect to the documents where it appears.

### Tools selected for the assignment

The approach accommodates tools such as ChatGPT, NotebookLM, Claude, Gemini, and Copilot. The choice depends on the source material, access requirements, and work being performed.

Private or local infrastructure is also part of the offering where it fits the assignment.

## Constraints And Decisions

I have kept the automation work focused on defined tasks: a known trigger, a bounded set of sources, a specific output, and a person responsible for reviewing it.

That could mean routing incoming material, processing documents, monitoring selected sources, or assembling a brief. Defining those boundaries makes the workflow easier to understand and maintain.

The research process also needs to distinguish what a source says from what has actually been established. Finding a statement in a document does not, by itself, make that statement true.

## Deliverables

The project includes the public website, service descriptions, training offerings, blog content, and the [Latest Signals section](https://haddamresearchgroup.com/signals/), which presents source attributed research briefings.

The service descriptions also outline potential engagement outputs, including workflow playbooks, evidence logs, timelines, research packets, and supporting tools.

## Outcome

Haddam Research Group gave me a dedicated place to develop and explain this work. It brings research, training, documentation, and selected automation into one offering.

The direction is practical: help people handle their information more consistently, retain the context behind their findings, and produce work they can return to and review.

## Tools And Methods

The approach combines AI research tools, source verification, document extraction, structured records, Markdown, Obsidian, and workflow automation. Private infrastructure and custom scripts are options where the work calls for them.
