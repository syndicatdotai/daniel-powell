# Using AI to Workshop Business Ideas and Build My Knowledge Base

## LinkedIn Summary

I use AI quite a bit to brainstorm business ideas, improvements to my current business, skills I could develop, and potential new ventures. I'm constantly bouncing ideas off it. Most of them don't go anywhere, but I enjoy the process.

I'll workshop the potential, the addressable market, the competitors, and the broader business landscape. Part of that is seeing whether I can identify an edge I might have if I decided to pursue the opportunity.

Sometimes the conversation stays close to construction, looking at workflows or different materials. Other times it takes me into industries that have very little to do with what I'm currently doing.

It's usually an exercise in exploring the idea. Even when I don't take it any further, I generally learn something about the industry or how that part of the world operates.

I usually save a snapshot of the discussion for future reference. There's often information I want to keep, even if I'm not going to pursue the business idea, and that can go into my Obsidian knowledge base.

## Overview

I regularly use AI to think through business ideas. Some conversations concern improvements to my existing business, updates to the way I work, or skills I could develop. Others involve entirely new ventures.

Most of these ideas do not progress beyond the conversation. I enjoy exploring them, and the discussion often teaches me something I would not otherwise have encountered. An idea can remain an exercise while the information I learn from it becomes something worth keeping.

## Role

I bring the interests, questions, and ideas into the conversation. AI gives me something to bounce them off as I work through the potential and consider whether an opportunity fits what I could offer.

One of the questions I keep returning to is whether I might have an edge. I want to understand the business landscape well enough to consider where my own experience or capabilities could fit.

## Scope Of Work

The discussions cover potential new businesses as well as changes within the business I already operate. That includes possible workflow improvements, skills, construction methods, and materials.

They also extend into industries that are completely different from my current work. Following an idea can become a way to learn how an unfamiliar industry functions, even when I have no immediate plan to enter it.

## Key Actions

### Explore the potential

I use the conversation to work through what a business idea might involve and whether there is an opportunity worth looking at more closely. The process gives me room to ask questions and keep developing the thought.

### Consider the market and competitors

I discuss the addressable market, existing competitors, and the broader business landscape. Those subjects help me examine the idea in the context of the industry rather than considering it in isolation.

### Look for a possible edge

As the discussion develops, I consider whether there is something I could bring to the opportunity. Identifying a potential edge is part of the exploration, rather than a conclusion I assume at the beginning.

### Keep useful information

I usually save a snapshot for future reference. The information worth keeping can concern the original idea, a construction workflow, a material, or something I learned about an unfamiliar industry. These snapshots can become part of my Obsidian knowledge base.

## Important Features

The process is conversational. I am continually bouncing ideas off AI, and I enjoy the workshopping itself.

The range of subjects is also important. Conversations move between things connected to my current business and areas well outside it. That gives me opportunities to learn about how other industries and businesses operate.

## Constraints And Decisions

Most ideas stay at the exploratory stage. Considering a potential market or discussing competitors does not mean I have decided to start a business.

I use these conversations to explore possibilities and identify questions. A possible edge is something to examine further if I decide to pursue the idea.

## Deliverables

The usual retained output is a snapshot of the discussion for future reference. It preserves information I want to return to and gives that learning a place in my knowledge base.

## Outcome

I generally come away having learned something, even when the original idea goes no further. That may be a better understanding of an unfamiliar industry, a different construction workflow, information about materials, or another perspective on how a business operates.

The exploration is an ongoing part of how I use AI. New ventures are one subject within it, alongside improving my current work and developing my understanding of other fields.

## Tools And Methods

I use conversational AI for brainstorming and workshopping ideas, including discussion of market potential, competitors, business conditions, and a possible personal advantage. Obsidian provides the knowledge base where useful information from those conversations can be kept.

## Evidence And Limitations

This account describes my ongoing brainstorming and learning practice. The opportunities discussed remain potential opportunities unless I choose to develop them further. The saved information supports future reference and exploration; it does not represent a completed business launch.
