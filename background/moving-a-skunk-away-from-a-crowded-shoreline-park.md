# Moving a Skunk Away From a Crowded Shoreline Park

Daniel intervened when a skunk moved through a crowded shoreline park in Madison, Connecticut, in the middle of the day. The park included camping areas, a beach, and a children’s playground, placing the animal close to families and other visitors.

A commotion drew his attention to the campground, where the skunk was walking among people. Its behavior and proximity to the crowd raised concerns about a possible rabies exposure and prompted him to act.

Daniel approached the group, identified himself as a Connecticut-licensed Nuisance Wildlife Control Operator, and asked people to give him room. He explained the concern and worked to create space between the visitors and the animal.

Over the next several minutes, he guided the skunk away from the occupied campground toward a wooded section of the property. The immediate objective was to move it out of the crowd and give it a route away from people.

The skunk entered the woods and continued to retreat. That resolved the immediate encounter without capturing or physically handling the animal.

Daniel subsequently reported the incident and his actions to local police. He also included the encounter in his annual activity report to the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection.

The incident illustrates another side of wildlife-control experience: recognizing a developing problem in a public setting, communicating clearly with the people nearby, and taking measured action to separate an animal from a crowd. In this case, the response centered on creating space and directing the animal away from the park’s occupied areas.
