# Responding to a Rabid Coyote on a Connecticut Farm

Daniel responded to a rabid coyote attacking people and animals on a Connecticut farm. The animal had attacked livestock, charged passing vehicles, and bitten the woman who owned the property. Testing after the incident confirmed rabies.

The call came through a family member whose neighbors owned the farm. The property covered substantial acreage, including wooded ground beyond the farm buildings and livestock areas. That terrain became important when the injured animal retreated into the woods.

According to the account relayed to Daniel, both local and state police had been contacted but had declined to respond. His family member knew he was a Connecticut Nuisance Wildlife Control Operator and called him for help.

Daniel regarded the situation as outside his ordinary nuisance-wildlife assignments, but the attacks presented an immediate life-safety concern. He took an M&P15 chambered in 5.56mm and a Springfield Armory 9mm as backup and drove to the farm.

A passing vehicle had struck and injured the coyote before it retreated into the wooded portion of the property. With help from several people tracking the animal, Daniel entered the farm’s wooded acreage and located it. He used the M&P15 to dispatch the coyote, ending the immediate threat to people and livestock.

Daniel and the others disclosed their actions to the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection and the local police department. Local police subsequently came to the property and took possession of the deceased animal for testing, which confirmed that it was rabid.

The incident brought together an urgent call through a family connection, a dangerous animal on a large rural property, and Daniel’s decision to respond when assistance had not arrived. His involvement continued through reporting the incident and transferring the animal to authorities for testing.
