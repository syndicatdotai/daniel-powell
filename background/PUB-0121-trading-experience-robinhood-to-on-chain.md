# My Trading Experience: From Robinhood to On-Chain Markets

I started trading on Robinhood in 2016. At that point, I had not done much research into trading strategies. I stumbled into a method that I later learned was a Martingale-style strategy: adding exposure as a trade moved against me, looking for a reversal that would bring the position back into profit.

I exclusively traded gold-related products with triple-leveraged long and inverse exposure. I did not experiment with much else. Within a few months, I doubled my account.

My account was below the $25,000 threshold associated with the pattern-day-trader rules at the time. In practice, I worked around a limit of three day trades within a rolling five-business-day period.

That restriction shaped how I traded. If I opened a position and it moved into profit, I would sometimes buy the opposing inverse ETF to hedge the position overnight instead of closing the profitable trade that day. My intention was to preserve the gain by offsetting the directional exposure. At the next market open, I would close both positions.

I alternated between two pairs of leveraged long and inverse gold-related products. After closing one pair, I would move to the other pair rather than immediately trading the same instruments again. That was how I organized the activity around my understanding of the account restrictions.

The strategy worked until I got caught on the wrong side of a sustained move. I was new to trading and had not understood how quickly the exposure could grow when the market kept moving against me. In a couple of days, I essentially lost all the gains I had accumulated. I stopped trading for a while.

## Discovering Cryptocurrency in 2017

I discovered cryptocurrency in 2017 and started trading near the tail end of the bull market.

At first, I used centralized exchanges. I was not doing much decentralized trading or using self-custody. If I wanted exposure to a particular token, the process I understood was to find a centralized exchange that listed it and open an account there.

I ended up opening accounts on a considerable number of exchanges to access different assets and instruments. The ecosystem itself became fascinating to me.

Coinbase was my initial entry point. Once I began moving cryptocurrency between exchanges, I found Bitcoin slow and expensive for the transfers I was making. I did not have many alternatives available through my original setup, but I discovered that Litecoin was faster and cheaper for that purpose.

I started converting assets into Litecoin, transferring the Litecoin to another exchange, and then converting it into whatever currency I actually wanted to trade. I was learning how to move capital around the ecosystem as well as how to trade the assets.

During the 2017 bull market, I accumulated approximately **$40,000 in unrealized gains** on an initial investment of about $7,000. I do not attribute that entirely to trading skill. Making money at the end of 2017 was relatively easy because so much of the market was going up.

In early 2018, I started losing a substantial amount of those profits. I eventually closed my positions with a 100% gain on my initial investment. I was still ahead, but I had given back a large portion of the account’s peak value.

I stepped away because I did not fully understand what was happening.

## Returning During the Late-2018 Decline

When Bitcoin fell from around $6,000 toward $3,000, I began putting money back into the market and accumulating cryptocurrency.

I did not buy as much as I would have liked in hindsight, but that period got me interested again. Around the same time, I also began experimenting with foreign exchange, commodities, and futures, although I did not do much with those markets then.

I continued trading through the period leading into the COVID crash in March 2020.

During that crash, the atmosphere felt catastrophic. From what I was seeing and hearing, it seemed as though people throughout the industry thought the market might be finished.

I had purchases lined up and was ready to act, but I did not have enough liquid capital available to make meaningful additional purchases. I could see an opportunity and had the orders prepared, but I could not fund them at the size I wanted.

## Margin Trading on Kraken

Around that period, I became more active and started using cryptocurrency margin trading. Kraken was one of the exchanges where I could trade with leverage.

I carried some of the same Martingale-style approaches from my Robinhood trading into cryptocurrency. I would add to positions and look for the reversal.

With a relatively small account, I traded hundreds of thousands of dollars in cumulative volume, going both long and short. That was turnover across repeated trades, rather than the size of the account itself.

When Bitcoin broke its previous highs in late 2020, I made a good amount of money relative to what I had invested. I traded margin longs heavily, but I also took shorts.

I actually made more money shorting during parts of that bull market than I did taking long positions.

I watched for moves that looked overheated. When the market pushed up sharply, I would average into a short position and try to catch the snapback. Sometimes my entries were in the upward wicks where shorts were being squeezed out or liquidated.

These were usually short-lived trades. I was trying to capture the rapid movement back after an extension, rather than hold a short through the entire bull market.

My interpretation was that the abrupt moves temporarily stretched the spread and left gaps in the order book before liquidity filled back in. I watched those movements and tried to take advantage of the reversal.

## The Coinbase Direct Listing and My April 2021 Short

As the market kept rising, I became less profitable. I did not understand why prices were going so high, and I was increasingly trading from a position of disbelief.

I started looking for shorts. I watched patterns on the daily chart, including repeated tests of highs and formations that looked like triple tops.

I opened a Bitcoin short the day before Coinbase’s direct listing on April 14, 2021. My view was that the listing could mark a market top.

I put a substantial amount of the money I had available into that position.

Before the listing, the market was squeezing shorts and looked bullish. I had entered too early for the size I was carrying. When the position came back near break-even, I closed it for a very small gain.

That trade was positioned around the April peak, before the substantial decline that followed. It was not the final high of the entire 2021 cycle; Bitcoin went higher later that year.

I had identified the turn I was looking for, but I did not execute the trade in a way that allowed me to stay in it. My sizing, conviction, and ability to tolerate the movement against me were not aligned.

There were other developments around that broader period that I associated with the change in sentiment, including privacy-coin delistings and Tesla-related Bitcoin news. Those were part of what I was watching, rather than a single explanation for the decline.

## The July 2021 Trade I Did Not Take

Around July 2021, I watched a three-impulse move down that made the market look as though it could break much lower.

I prepared a Bitcoin purchase using my entire trading account at five-times leverage.

I never executed it.

I had the order ready and was considering pressing the buy button, but the amount of exposure was too large. If the market broke down further, the loss could have wiped me out. I could not tolerate that possibility.

The setup was around the July low, before the market recovered. Once again, I had been watching a significant turning point, but I had sized the proposed trade in a way that prevented me from acting.

I largely sat out the later-2021 rally. At the time, I associated some of the renewed activity with FTX’s growing presence and influence in the market. That was my interpretation of what I was seeing.

I did not catch a substantial short near the later high either.

## Stepping Away in 2022

Between January and April 2022, I watched what looked like a bear flag forming on the weekly chart.

My assessment was that the market was heading into an extended bearish period. I largely tuned out for about a year.

I had used FTX before its collapse and had traded there within weeks of the failure, but I did not have money on the exchange when it collapsed. It was a close call.

My interest in Solana had started before that. FTX’s involvement in the Solana ecosystem was part of what initially drew my attention to it.

By the time FTX failed, my activity had largely moved on-chain, using Phantom on Solana. I also had some on-chain Ethereum exposure, but the gas fees made the kind of trading I wanted to do difficult to justify.

## The False ETF Story That Got My Attention

One event that brought my attention back was a false report that a spot Bitcoin ETF had been approved.

The report that matches this episode was published in October 2023. The market shot up on short time frames, then reversed after the story was exposed as false.

What interested me was the strength of the initial reaction.

Even though the news was wrong, the buying was real. My interpretation was that there was more interest waiting on the sidelines than I had appreciated. A headline had brought that demand into the market almost immediately.

I began studying technical analysis more seriously again and researching the expanding blockchain ecosystem. There were many more chains, including layer-one networks and different layers built around them.

## How I Made Money Trading NFTs

My profitable NFT trading came from finding mispriced opportunities.

I followed collections that I considered valuable, watched their prices, and set alerts. If there was a flash crash and an NFT became available significantly below what I considered fair value, I would act quickly.

The discount had to be substantial. I was not simply buying because a collection was popular or because I expected it to become the next big thing.

I bought low offers during those dislocations and flipped the NFTs when the prices recovered. There were several collections where I was able to do that successfully.

Overall, I made money trading NFTs.

The NFTs I bought to hold for the long term were a different story. Those holdings essentially became worthless. The money I made came from buying and flipping, particularly when a collection I was already watching briefly became mispriced.

## Working On-Chain and Choosing Solana

As I returned to more active trading, I made a significant amount of money on-chain.

That also produced a substantial tax bill. I withdrew money to cover the taxes and paid them when they came due.

I experimented across several chains, but Solana became my main ecosystem. By 2024, I preferred it to Ethereum for much of what I was doing.

Ethereum’s gas fees were a major reason. I also disliked how fragmented the experience felt: liquidity spread across different networks and layers, competing communities, and factions arguing with each other online.

That was my assessment as someone trying to use the ecosystem regularly.

On Solana, I experimented with single-sided and two-sided liquidity, opening liquidity-pool positions, borrowing, lending, and on-chain leveraged trading. I also explored NFT staking and token-creation processes through pump.fun.

I used Phantom for self-custody and Jupiter for aggregated on-chain swaps. My interest extended beyond taking a directional position in a coin. I wanted to understand how the applications worked and how funds moved through them.

## Taking Money Out After the 2024 Election

After Donald Trump won the election in November 2024, the market accelerated, but I became hesitant.

I had already started taking money out of the ecosystem. I did not want to repeat the earlier experience of watching a large gain disappear.

I wanted to leave while prices were rising.

My assessment was that much of the demand had been pulled forward. The election and the expectations around it had given people a reason to buy, but I questioned how many new buyers would still be available after that move.

That was my market view, rather than something I could know with certainty.

## My Final MELANIA Trade

I spotted the TRUMP token relatively early, but I thought it might be fake, so I did not participate.

Then I started hearing that a MELANIA token might launch. I paid close attention to social media and other online activity so I could identify the launch quickly.

Before entering, I configured higher transaction-priority fees and a high slippage tolerance. I wanted the transaction to execute during what I expected to be a competitive launch.

I committed approximately 50% of my available trading balance. By that point, I had already withdrawn a portion of my funds from the ecosystem.

I bought MELANIA within a few minutes of its January 19, 2025 launch.

Shortly after I entered, activity became heavily congested and the Solana trading experience slowed down. My transaction had already gone through with the execution settings I had chosen.

I stayed glued to the computer for approximately four hours, watching the one-minute chart.

It was not the most money I had ever made on a coin, but it was the best-executed trade I had made. I had prepared for the entry, watched it continuously, and stayed focused on the exit.

Eventually, I saw short-term topping patterns and got the feeling that it was time to leave. I sold the entire position within approximately 10% of the top I was watching.

By inauguration day, January 20, I was flat across my trading positions. That was the end of my active trading.

## Why I Stayed Out

The market declined after that period, and I initially felt justified in my decision to leave.

What I did not anticipate was the subsequent rebound through part of 2025. I did not participate because I no longer saw an opportunity that interested me enough to return.

I was also becoming cynical about the ecosystem.

I saw scams, constant token launches, and what people called extraction from the trenches. I watched the activity around protocols, Telegram trading bots, aggregators, and platforms such as Dex Screener. I became increasingly focused on the money being collected through fees and trading activity, and on what that meant for the people taking the other side.

The industry began to feel less convincing to me. My interest in actively trading it diminished.

As of November 26, 2026, I have not resumed trading since my January 2025 exit. That is approximately 22 months.

I still follow prices almost daily. I keep up with market developments and remain interested in cryptocurrency. I also watch the broader stock market, including Nvidia and the relationship between technology stocks and speculative assets.

I currently have a bearish bias. I do not know exactly what will change that view, but I would like to accumulate cryptocurrency again when I see conditions that justify it.

For now, I am watching and waiting.
