## Railing Generator and manufacturing documentation

**Role:** Field measurer, shop drafter, application developer, and working user.

Daniel developed Railing Generator around recurring shop-drawing assignments for a railing manufacturer. Manual drafting supplied the initial understanding of post placement, rail relationships, spindle spacing, dimensions, and the information required by the shop.

The application uses a shared drawing model for browser input and a Node.js command-line workflow. Voice instructions can supply field measurements, rail configuration, landing conditions, and printed notes. Codex prepares the inputs; the application calculates the drawing from those inputs.

Editable JSON projects preserve the decisions behind each drawing. Outputs include DXF for AutoCAD, vector SVG sheets, rendered images, and component cut sheets. Revised measurements can be carried through the same model rather than recreated separately in each output.

By November 25, 2026, Daniel had reduced drawing preparation from approximately 45–60 minutes in AutoCAD to approximately 30–90 seconds for configurations the generator already supports, once field measurements and layout are known.

New field conditions guide development. Repeated details become reusable features; unusual one-off details may remain manual AutoCAD work. Generated dimensions and outputs still receive review for the particular job.

## AI workflows and operational automation

Daniel built an Obsidian-centered operating system for keeping project context available across field work, correspondence, drafting, scheduling, and business administration. Markdown records preserve the underlying history, while reusable instructions and scripts handle bounded tasks.

Examples include daily-note creation, email-to-vault intake, propagation of field notes to relevant records, task scans, document preparation, research intake, publication queues, and log maintenance. Some workflows are scheduled; others are explicitly initiated when needed.

His approach separates interpretation from repeatable computation. AI can interpret a voice instruction or organize source material, while a script or application performs the defined operation. Review gates, retained originals, bounded retries, duplicate checks, and verification of the destination reduce the chance of treating an attempted operation as completed work.

Remote and voice workflows extend this system beyond the desk. Daniel uses field dictation to capture observations, prepare drawing inputs, initiate document work, and preserve decisions while the relevant conditions are still in front of him.

## Reality capture and spatial documentation

Daniel designed and maintains a workflow that turns a suitable phone video into a Gaussian splat and an interactive browser viewer. His role includes capture planning, toolchain selection, installation and compatibility troubleshooting, workflow development, quality gates, and review of the delivered scene.

The pipeline uses FFmpeg for media inspection and frame extraction, COLMAP for camera reconstruction, Brush for Gaussian-splat training, SplatTransform for delivery conversion, and SuperSplat for browser viewing. Camera reconstruction is checked before full training, and outputs are reviewed before publication and handoff.

The established pipeline provides an approximately 30-minute path from suitable source video to a live scene when the established workflow passes its gates without recovery work. Daniel developed capture and camera-solve checks to identify motion, reflections, insufficient coverage, and reconstruction failures before full training.

Additional spatial work includes LiDAR terrain review and QGIS-assisted site-planning exhibits.
