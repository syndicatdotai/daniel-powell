# From a Personal Bed Bug Problem to Running a Commercial Service Business

My introduction to the bed bug business came through dealing with an infestation ourselves. We brought bed bugs home through a public library book, and we were not in a financial position to hire a professional to resolve the problem.

I started researching. I looked into treatment methods, equipment, products, and ways to tackle the infestation myself. After eradicating the problem, I opened a business around what I had learned.

That personal experience shaped how I connected with clients. When someone was frightened, disgusted, or overwhelmed, I could tell them directly: I had been through it myself. I understood how disruptive it was and how much of the experience extended beyond the physical presence of insects.

That understanding became an important part of the service. I could discuss the problem without judgment and relate to people who were dealing with something that had made their own surroundings uncomfortable.

## Building a focused business

I approached a large commercial client in the same area and secured a contract that ran for approximately two years. That became the primary account throughout the operation of the business.

I handled the entire operation: sales, marketing, scheduling, service delivery, and customer support. The work included recurring appointments, coordinating with building management, and carrying out service discreetly.

At the same time, I was running my construction businesses and providing nuisance wildlife control services. Bed bug work fit alongside those activities as another service connected to buildings and the problems people encounter within them.

I fielded other calls and looked at additional opportunities, but I did not pursue expansion beyond what made sense alongside my other work. One primary commercial client gave the business a defined scope that I could personally manage.

## Choosing the approach and positioning the service

Speed to market influenced my choice of methods. I began studying the pesticide licensing route, but the qualification and testing requirements affected how I wanted to establish the business as a solo operator.

I shifted toward an alternative-treatment approach centered on equipment such as a Cryonite machine for direct-contact dry-ice treatment. My research and historical methods also included isopropyl alcohol and diatomaceous earth.

I positioned the service around a different approach from conventional pesticide treatments. I wanted customers to understand why they might choose my service and what distinguished it from other available options.

I also priced it as a premium service. Some prospective jobs did not move forward because my price was higher than the customer wanted to pay. I accepted that. I was already balancing other businesses, and I wanted the work I took on to justify the time, equipment, and responsibility involved.

## How the service led to construction work

The bed bug service also led to related construction contracts. Some rooms were heavily infested, and the client wanted them remodeled after rounds of treatment. My construction experience allowed me to take on that work as well.

The work included removing carpets, clearing out room contents, disassembling bed frames, and preparing mattresses and other items for disposal. Because the building remained actively occupied, the removal process required careful planning. Other people were still using the surrounding spaces, and everything coming out of the affected rooms had to travel through the building.

Containing the material during removal became a significant part of the operation. Items had to be double-bagged, mattresses wrapped in plastic, and larger furnishings broken down and contained for transport. The objective was to avoid carrying bed bugs or eggs into shared spaces along the removal route.

That made the assignment more involved than simply stripping out a room. The sequence of disassembly, wrapping, handling, and removal had to account for both the affected rooms and the occupied areas beyond them.

These contracts brought together my service work, construction capabilities, and experience coordinating work around building occupants. They also showed how one specialized service could create opportunities for related work that I was already equipped to perform.

## Seeing buildings differently

One of the most lasting lessons came from seeing how connected the interior spaces of a building could be.

I already understood framing and construction assemblies through my trade work. Bed bug investigations gave me a much finer appreciation of the small pathways between rooms, neighboring apartments, and floors.

On properties I visited, the problem could extend sideways and vertically through the building. A treatment in one apartment did not necessarily mean the surrounding spaces were isolated from the issue. It made me look more closely at the difference between a wall or floor appearing closed and the assembly actually limiting movement through its concealed spaces.

That observation also prompted questions from my construction background about penetrations, separation between occupancies, and firestopping. Insect movement alone does not establish a fire-rating defect, but it gave me another reason to pay attention to openings and concealed connections rather than judging an assembly only by its finished surface.

## What carried forward

The business connected several parts of my experience: independent research, equipment selection, commercial contracts, hands-on service, and communication with people under stress.

It also reinforced the value of keeping a business deliberately focused. I personally supported one primary commercial account while continuing my construction and wildlife work.

The experience stayed with me in two ways. It strengthened my ability to connect with clients facing an unfamiliar and upsetting problem, and it changed how closely I examine the small connections within residential and commercial buildings.
