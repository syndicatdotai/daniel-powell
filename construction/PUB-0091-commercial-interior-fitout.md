# Coordinating Partitions, Glass, and Acoustics in a Commercial Fit-Out

**Public project ID:** PUB-0091

At Infinity Group, I worked on a commercial interior fit-out that involved adapting a large open space into enclosed rooms. My responsibilities included establishing the schedule, buying out subcontractors, and providing on-site project management.

The scope brought together partitions, doors, glazing, ceilings, lighting, and existing building services. These elements affected the same spaces and needed to be coordinated across several trades.

Sound separation between rooms was one of the design requirements. Wall height and the conditions above the ceiling were part of that consideration, with some partitions specified to extend to the underside of the deck. Glazed doors and sidelights added details that needed to work with the surrounding partitions and openings.

Existing conditions also shaped the work. Some glazing was to remain, while new openings and partitions had to fit into the surrounding space. Door and hardware requirements needed to be considered alongside those conditions rather than treated as isolated purchases.

My subcontractor buyout and scheduling responsibilities brought me into the coordination of those trade packages. Glazing, door hardware, ceilings, and sprinkler work each had their own requirements, but they all contributed to the same interior conversion.

The design team defined the layout and construction requirements. My role was to organize the subcontracted work and provide on-site project management, connecting the project schedule with the practical requirements of the fit-out.

This project illustrates the coordination involved in dividing an existing commercial space into rooms. A partition affects more than the room boundary: its height, openings, acoustic requirements, and relationship to building services all need attention. Those relationships were central to organizing the work.
