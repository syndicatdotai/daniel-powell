# Testing the Feasibility of a Childcare Addition

A general contractor commissioned a feasibility study for an addition to an existing childcare facility. The starting information was the client's intended use, a requested building size, and the contractor's general direction.

Checking the proposed footprint against zoning and childcare space requirements revealed that the requested size would not support the intended program. The study established the need for a larger addition and developed a building concept around that finding.

### Turning the program into a design

The building design included room layouts, exterior elevations, and construction details. Existing finish selections provided continuity with the original facility. An engineer handled site grading associated with roof-water management, alongside the architectural design work.

Floor plans, sections, door and window schedules, a reflected ceiling plan, and renderings gave the client several ways to evaluate the proposal. The package made the spatial implications of the intended operation visible before committing to further development.

### Supporting research and drawing review

AI supported note taking, research into childcare regulations, and investigation of building assembly methods. Drawing checks covered spelling, consistent commentary, and matching dates and revision numbers across the sheets.

The feasibility package established the space required for the proposed operation and documented a building concept for the contractor and client to evaluate.
