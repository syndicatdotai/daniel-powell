# Coordinating Crane Access and Construction of a Multistory Fire-Rated Shaft for Verizon Infrastructure

**Public project ID:** PUB-0129

A commercial construction assignment required building a multistory fire-rated shaft enclosure around Verizon infrastructure. The chase extended between two floors and contained components that continued through the roof.

The assignment combined framing, drywall installation, and taping with the logistics of getting materials to the building’s top floor. Crane access had to be coordinated with its primary user, and material loads needed to fit within the existing schedule.

My responsibilities included the site visit, access and loading coordination, and construction of the enclosure. Following the site review and logistical arrangements, the installation was completed the next day.

## Planning Around the Building and the Work

The enclosure’s location shaped how the assignment needed to be organized.

This was a vertical shaft spanning two floors, with Verizon components continuing through the roof. The construction scope was the fire-rated enclosure surrounding that infrastructure.

Before installation could begin, framing materials and drywall needed to reach the upper-floor work area. Crane-assisted loading provided the means to move those materials into position.

The site visit connected the construction requirements with the practical conditions of the building. Planning included both the enclosure work and the access arrangements needed to support it.

That preparation established the sequence: coordinate the loading opportunity, have materials available, move them to the upper floor, and proceed with construction.

## Coordinating Crane Access

The crane already had a primary user whose work governed its availability.

Loading therefore required communication and scheduling. Access had to be arranged around an existing operation, with the framing and drywall loads coordinated for an agreed window.

My role included organizing that access for the shaft-wall assignment. The material-loading schedule and installation schedule needed to work together so the construction could proceed once the materials reached the work area.

This is a recurring feature of commercial construction: responsibility for a defined scope often includes managing dependencies outside the installation itself. Equipment availability, material delivery, and access controlled by others can all determine when the work can begin.

For this project, crane coordination was part of preparing the assignment for execution.

## Constructing the Fire-Rated Shaft Enclosure

The construction work included framing, drywall installation, and taping to form the multistory fire-rated shaft enclosure.

The shaft surrounded Verizon components passing between floors and continuing through the roof. Its vertical configuration made the enclosure part of the building’s floor-to-floor construction, extending beyond a partition contained within a single room.

The assignment required bringing the materials, access, and installation sequence together around that configuration.

With the loading arrangements established and the materials moved to the upper floor, the enclosure work was completed the following day.

The short installation period rested on preparation completed in advance. The site visit and scheduling work made it possible to arrive with the logistical requirements already addressed.

## Connecting Logistics with Field Execution

The project brought together two parts of construction delivery that are often described separately: coordinating the work and physically building it.

The coordination covered the site review, communication with the crane’s primary user, and scheduling material loads. The field scope covered framing, drywall, and taping for the shaft enclosure.

Keeping those responsibilities connected allowed the planning to follow the needs of the installation. Material access was addressed as part of the construction sequence, with the upper-floor work area and multistory enclosure guiding the arrangements.

The assignment demonstrates how a focused construction scope can require broader operational judgment. The enclosure was the finished work, while crane access and material loading were essential steps in delivering it.

## A Complete Construction Assignment

This project reflects an approach that carries responsibility from the initial site visit through installation.

The work required understanding the shaft enclosure, organizing materials, coordinating shared equipment, and completing the framing and drywall scope. Each step supported the next.

The result was a completed multistory fire-rated shaft enclosure around Verizon infrastructure, delivered through advance logistical planning and direct construction work.
