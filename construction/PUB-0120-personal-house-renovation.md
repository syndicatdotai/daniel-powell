# Rebuilding a House While Living Through the Renovation

**Public project ID:** PUB-0120

## Overview

I purchased a house dating to the early 1900s, renovated it extensively while living in it, and later sold it for twice the original purchase price.

The project was a substantial reconstruction of the interior, taking the house back to its framing and rebuilding the spaces, services, and finishes. It combined an open-plan first floor, structural alterations, electrical and plumbing renewal, restored hardwood floors, renovated bathrooms, and the conversion of two porches into finished rooms.

Most of the work was completed personally over approximately three to four years, with occasional help from friends and family. Licensed tradespeople handled specialist electrical and plumbing work, and the roofing was contracted out. The project brought ownership, construction, material purchasing, trade coordination, and day-to-day living into the same undertaking.

## Opening the Floor Plan and Reworking the Structure

The first-floor renovation removed interior walls to create an open floor plan. Engineered beams replaced the interrupted wall supports, with basement footing improvements, columns, and squash blocking forming part of the structural work.

Those changes extended beyond the appearance of the rooms. Opening the interior required attention to the support below it as well as the framing above. The basement work and blocking were part of carrying the revised arrangement through the building rather than treating the new beams as isolated additions.

The upstairs room configuration was retained. Work there concentrated on new wiring, insulation, drywall, restored flooring, and a renovated bathroom. This allowed the project to make substantial changes where they improved the layout while retaining the arrangement of the upper floor.

## Renewing the Electrical, Plumbing, and Heating Distribution

The electrical work removed the old wiring, renewed the panel, and introduced new wiring throughout the house. Plumbing alterations removed the old branch configurations back to the existing stack and connected new arrangements to it.

The original radiators were retained, with new PEX distribution lines and individual flow-control valves in the basement. This provided a way to adjust the flow to each radiator and tune the distribution of heat more precisely.

A high-efficiency, gas-fired tankless water heater was installed in the basement. Retaining the radiators while renewing their distribution was part of an approach that combined useful existing components with updated services.

Licensed tradespeople completed the more complex electrical and plumbing work. My contribution included material procurement, wire routing, basic plumbing work, and the preparatory labor around those trade packages, alongside the broader renovation.

## A Kitchen Organized Around an Angled Cooking Area

The kitchen used an unconventional arrangement: the stove was set diagonally to the main room geometry, with the island aligned parallel to it.

That relationship created a wider area at the cooking position and gave the room a different working layout from a conventional arrangement of parallel walls and counters. The angle was a deliberate part of how the kitchen was organized.

The work included cabinet installation, countertops, and tile finishes. Kitchen and bathroom construction were carried through from the underlying alterations to the finished surfaces, including a new upstairs vanity, revised shower configuration, and tilework.

## Converting the Porches Into Finished Living Space

The rear porch became a laundry room with a washer, dryer, and sink. The front porch became a finished entry room with a coat closet and space for shoes.

Both conversions received high ceilings and Level 5 drywall finishes. Roof-framing alterations included removing collar ties and adding bracing from the rafters back to the original structure. High framing members were wrapped in drywall, incorporating them into the finished room design.

In the laundry room, LEDs mounted above the drywall-wrapped beams directed light upward across the ceiling. The beam treatment and concealed lighting made the overhead construction part of the room's visual character.

The Level 5 finish was an important part of that treatment. Smooth ceiling surfaces and upward-directed light were considered together, with particular attention given to the quality of the drywall finishing. The front and rear rooms received spray-applied white eggshell paint on both the drywall and trim, carrying the same color and sheen across the finished surfaces.

## Restoring Existing Materials and Adding Custom Details

The original hardwood floors were retained on both main levels. They were sanded and stained, with polyurethane applied to the upstairs flooring.

New insulation and drywall were installed throughout the renovated interior. Alongside the general finishing work, the project included custom drywall installations and decorative drywall details.

The combination of retained flooring, new finishes, and custom work gave the renovation continuity with the existing house while allowing substantial changes to its layout and interior character.

## Sequencing the Work Around Ownership and Occupancy

The attic was completed first, providing the starting point for living in the property while the rest of the renovation continued.

Living on site reduced the cost of maintaining a separate rental while also carrying a mortgage on a house under construction. It also made the renovation part of everyday life, with the work progressing over several years rather than through a single concentrated construction period.

The project subsequently received its certificate of occupancy. Additional work continued later, including siding and roofing updates.

An exterior sewer-line replacement addressed tree-root intrusion into the existing clay piping. This was a separate infrastructure issue from the interior plumbing renewal and became another part of maintaining and improving the property over the ownership period.

## Outcome

The renovation rebuilt much of the house's interior while retaining useful features, including the hardwood floors, original radiators, and upstairs room arrangement. Structural alterations opened the first floor, the porches became finished rooms, and the electrical, plumbing, and heating distribution received substantial updates.

The house was later sold for twice its original purchase price. The project represented several years of personally performed construction and finish work, combined with material purchasing, licensed-trade coordination, and continued improvements during ownership.
