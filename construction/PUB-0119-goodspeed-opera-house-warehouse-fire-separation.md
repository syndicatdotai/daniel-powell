# Goodspeed Opera House: Assembly Selection and Installation for a Warehouse Fire Separation

Around 2012, Daniel Powell helped select and install a substantial fire-separation wall at a newly acquired Goodspeed Opera House warehouse in East Haddam, Connecticut. The facility was being prepared to accommodate offices alongside storage for costumes and production supplies.

The storage area had a large, hangar-like interior with high ceilings. Plans called for a multilevel garment-storage structure with racks, catwalks, and stairs to provide access to costumes stored at different elevations. The local fire marshal required a four-hour fire-rated assembly separating the planned storage area from the offices.

The general contractor hired Daniel with the basic design intent established but without a preferred wall assembly. Daniel brought practical construction knowledge to the decision, guiding the contractor through an assembly approach and then carrying out the framing and gypsum-board installation.

## Helping the contractor select the assembly

Daniel's involvement extended beyond installing a predetermined wall system. He helped the contractor choose the construction method for the required separation, drawing on his understanding of shaft-wall components and multilayer gypsum assemblies.

The installation used one-inch-thick gypsum core-board panels, two feet wide by ten feet long, fitted between H-shaped shaft-wall framing sections. These were followed by three layers of gypsum board using sheets four feet wide by twelve feet long and five-eighths of an inch thick. Selecting the approach also meant working through how the wall could be assembled progressively in the building's tall interior.

That connection between material selection and installation experience was central to Daniel's contribution. He could explain how the components fit together and then perform the work needed to build the assembly.

## Framing and installing a tall wall

The wall required several stages of framing and panel installation. Daniel operated a man lift to reach the elevated work areas, sliding the core boards between the shaft-wall framing sections as the wall progressed.

The sequence mattered: framing and core-panel placement had to advance together before the subsequent gypsum-board layers could be installed. The height of the work added access and material-handling demands throughout the installation.

The project required approximately **175 core-board panels and 250 sheets of gypsum board**. Framing and board installation took several weeks, making this a sustained specialty installation with a substantial volume of material.

## Combining technical guidance with hands-on execution

Daniel contributed at both the planning and installation stages. He guided the general contractor through assembly selection, worked out the staged construction approach, operated the lift, and performed the framing and board installation.

The Goodspeed Opera House project demonstrates his ability to translate a project requirement into practical construction work, combining knowledge of specialty wall systems with the hands-on experience to install them.
