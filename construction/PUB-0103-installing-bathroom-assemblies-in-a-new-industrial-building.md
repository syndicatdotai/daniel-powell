# Installing Bathroom Assemblies in a New Industrial Building

Two bathrooms in a new industrial building required heavy-gauge metal framing, truss installation, fire-rated plywood, and drywall. The general contractor engaged me to carry out that installation scope from an engineer-designed framing package.

The work used familiar assembly methods, with equipment and sequencing adapted to the heavier materials and available access.

### Planning the installation sequence

Heavy-gauge steel required specialty cutting equipment. Framing and trusses were installed with the plywood and drywall in the assigned scope.

Tight clearance beside the building's exterior wall made sequencing important. Drywall in the narrow space had to be installed as the framing progressed, while the surface remained accessible.

### Coordinating field conditions

Checking installed conditions against the drawings revealed discrepancies needing attention. Plumbing positions differed from the blueprints in places where the services and new framing met.

Those findings became coordination items for the project team. Engineering design, plumbing work, and the assigned installation scope each had their own responsibilities.

I performed the framing, truss, plywood, and drywall installation, coordinating the sequence with access and the installed services.
