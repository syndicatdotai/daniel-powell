# Managing Construction Inside an Active Retirement Community

**Public project ID:** PUB-0088

During my time with Infinity Group around 2019–2020, I worked on converting existing office space into apartments inside an operating retirement community. Residents and staff continued to use the surrounding building while construction was underway.

My responsibilities included subcontractor buyout, scheduling, field coordination, and communication with the client. I worked with the design and architectural teams to provide existing dimensions and field conditions, supplementing those discussions with hand-drawn sketches and diagrams.

Working inside an occupied community meant coordinating with security, management, maintenance, and residents. I personally organized the signage directing people around the construction area, including detours and hallway closures. Keeping routes understandable was part of managing construction within a building that residents and staff continued to use.

I also organized walks around the construction site throughout the day to evaluate containment safety and the condition of barriers. These checks were part of my active oversight of the separation between the work area and the occupied building. My responsibilities extended from planning the work to reviewing the site conditions as that work progressed.

I held weekly client meetings and daily safety meetings with contractors. That gave the project a regular rhythm for discussing progress, upcoming work, and safety considerations. It also connected the broader project schedule with the activities taking place on site each day.

I managed fire watch and coordinated sprinkler and HVAC shutdowns needed for new construction connections. That work involved both the contractors making the connections and the people responsible for the building's ongoing operation.

The construction sequence had several dependencies. Flooring needed to precede base trim. Millwork and countertops affected plumbing finishes. Rough work and above-ceiling work had inspection stages to account for. Doors, ceilings, sprinkler finishes, and laundry-room work also had to fit into the schedule.

Alongside that coordination, I supplied field measurements and sketches to help the designers and architects understand the existing conditions. My role connected the design team, subcontractors, client, and building staff throughout the work I managed.

This project brought together many parts of on-site project management: purchasing subcontracted work, sequencing trades, communicating field conditions, organizing service interruptions, and maintaining routes around construction. In an occupied retirement community, all of those responsibilities had to be considered together.
