# Reconstructing Curved Drywall After a Rejected Installation

A drywall company that periodically calls on me for specialty carpentry brought me in to repair curved walls after an installation was rejected. The assignment involved diagnosing the surface problems and reconstructing three curved sections.

Removing the drywall exposed inadequate backing along the radius. Too few studs supported the sheets as they bent, producing visible angles where a continuous curve was intended.

### Establishing the geometry

A template laid out on the floor and moved into position established a physical reference for the curve. Comparing it with the blueprints showed that the framed shape also differed from the drawings. Both the geometry and backing concerns were raised with the general contractor.

Removing the existing sheets exposed the underlying conditions for assessment and addressed the contractor's concerns about retaining the earlier installation.

### Forming the new surface

Quarter-inch drywall was installed in layers to form the curve. The sheets were cut to length and laid face down, and joint compound was applied to their backs. After a few minutes, the moisture allowed the sheets to bend during placement.

Each sheet was lifted into position, gradually worked around the radius, and fastened to the studs with drywall screws. The template established the shape used to check the surface throughout the repair.

The three curved sections were reconstructed using practical carpentry and drywall methods. The work connected the intended geometry to the framing and finished surface.
