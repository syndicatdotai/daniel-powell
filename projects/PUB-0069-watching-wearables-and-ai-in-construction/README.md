# Watching Wearables and AI in Construction

## LinkedIn Summary

I've been experimenting with real time AI assistants in the field, including having AI keep track of measurements as I say them out loud. That's made me pay more attention to wearables and where they could fit into construction work.

The idea isn't new, but it feels more practical now. The hardware is moving closer to something I could use throughout a site visit, and I think AI will make it substantially easier to build the applications around it.

I'm interested in being able to describe what I'm looking at, record a measurement and what it relates to, and keep moving through the job without pulling out a screen for every note.

I've already been experimenting with that through voice. I'm watching how much of the same workflow could move into something I wear.

## Overview

My interest in wearables is coming from the work I've already been doing with real time AI assistants in the field. I've been experimenting with speaking measurements and observations aloud and having AI keep track of that information.

That gives me specific things to look for as wearable hardware and AI applications develop. I'm thinking about how they would fit into an ordinary site visit, where I'm moving around and looking at the work.

## Role

I take the measurements and describe the conditions. AI helps capture the information I provide. I'm using those experiments to think through where a wearable interface could fit.

## Scope Of Work

The current experiments involve voice interaction with AI in the field. Wearable integration is a direction I'm watching, rather than a completed system I'm already using.

The use case is capturing a measurement along with what it refers to, keeping related observations together, and making that information available for project documentation.

## Key Actions

I speak the information while I'm looking at the work. A measurement needs its context: what I measured and how it relates to the surrounding conditions.

I'm paying attention to the steps that still require a screen and considering whether a wearable interface could handle them during a visit.

## Important Features

Voice input, accessible audio responses, and continuity between related notes are the features I'm interested in. A wearable would need to fit the way I move through a job and preserve the relationship between an observation and the work it describes.

## Constraints And Decisions

The hardware and the application both matter. A device needs to be practical to wear, and the software needs to capture useful project information.

I think AI assisted application development will make it substantially easier to build and test software around these field workflows. That's my expectation for where this is heading.

## Deliverables

The current work produces captured field notes from spoken measurements and observations. A dedicated wearable application remains a possible next step.

## Outcome

The experiments have made me pay closer attention to wearables. I have specific tasks in mind when I look at the hardware: recording information as I go, retaining its context, and reducing the number of times I need to reach for a screen.

## Tools And Methods

The current method uses real time AI assistance and spoken field notes. Wearable interfaces and AI assisted software development are the areas I'm exploring next.

For general hardware context, Google's Android XR demonstration describes glasses with cameras, microphones and speakers for interacting with an AI assistant. That is a relevant device direction, rather than evidence of construction suitability. ## Evidence And Limitations

This account reflects my field experiments and my expectations for wearable applications. It does not establish widespread construction adoption or describe AI independently taking measurements. No particular wearable has been evaluated here for job site use.
