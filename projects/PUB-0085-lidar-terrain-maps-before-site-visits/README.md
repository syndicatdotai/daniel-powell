## Overview

I recently started making more use of publicly available LiDAR terrain mapping when researching properties. My initial experience was with Connecticut's mapping resources, where terrain features became easier to pick out than they were in ordinary aerial imagery.

Detailed elevation data is available across much of the United States through national and state mapping programs. The USGS 3D Elevation Program provides a way to explore that coverage and find available datasets. [USGS 3DEP](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program)

## Role

I use the mapping as part of my preliminary research for construction projects, comparing the terrain with aerial images and identifying questions to take into the first site visit.

## Scope Of Work

The focus is understanding the property before arriving: the shape of the land, changes in slope, possible access routes, and traces of earlier use that may be difficult to distinguish in an aerial photograph.

## Key Actions

- Find available terrain mapping for the property.
- Compare the ground surface with aerial imagery.
- Look for changes in slope and possible old roadbeds or trails.
- Identify areas to inspect and questions to resolve during the site visit.

## Important Features

LiDAR stands for light detection and ranging. Its elevation measurements can be processed into a bare earth digital elevation model, separating the ground surface from vegetation and structures. That makes the underlying terrain easier to examine. [USGS explanation of LiDAR and elevation models](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-difference-between-lidar-data-and-a-digital-elevation-model-dem)

For me, the useful part is being able to notice features that don't stand out in a normal overhead image. A wooded area can look fairly uniform in a photograph while the terrain view shows changes in grade or the outline of a possible old route through it.

## Constraints And Decisions

I use the maps to guide the initial investigation. A feature that resembles an old road or trail is something to check on site, and the age and detail of the available data matter when interpreting current conditions.

The mapping supports preliminary planning; field measurements and any required survey still establish the information needed for the actual work.

## Deliverables

A more informed starting point for the site visit, including terrain observations, possible access considerations, and specific areas to examine on the ground.

## Outcome

This has added another useful source of information to how I research a property. I can begin thinking through the terrain and access before the first visit, then compare those observations with what I find on site.

## Tools And Methods

Public LiDAR elevation data, bare earth terrain mapping, contour maps, aerial imagery, and comparison with field conditions. USGS LidarExplorer and The National Map provide access to available elevation data. [USGS data access](https://www.usgs.gov/the-national-map-data-delivery/gis-data-download)

## Evidence And Limitations

This account reflects my experience using Connecticut terrain mapping and my interest in its wider availability. Coverage, collection dates, and detail vary by location; national elevation coverage should not be read as identical LiDAR coverage for every property.
