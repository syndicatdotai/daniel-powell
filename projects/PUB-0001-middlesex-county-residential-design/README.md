## Overview

This residential project began with an existing design previously developed by Daniel Powell. The owner selected that design as the starting point and requested changes to adapt it to a different property, program, and construction approach.

The project evolved through several substantial revisions rather than a single drafting pass. Interior layouts changed, floor-plan and elevation information had to remain coordinated, the foundation strategy changed more than once, and municipal review generated a second layer of technical corrections. The work became a practical example of using an established design as a controlled starting point while still responding to project-specific requirements.

## Role

Daniel's documented role centered on residential planning, CAD-based drafting, drawing revision, permit-response coordination, and follow-up technical support. The record does not represent licensed design-professional or engineering services. Specialized professional services are coordinated separately when required by code, jurisdiction, or project conditions.

## Scope Of Work

The drawing scope included a cover sheet, coordinated floor plans, exterior elevations, foundation information, construction details, and code-related notes. Requested changes affected kitchen circulation, bedroom-suite planning, bathroom and laundry arrangements, exterior openings, and the relationship between the plans and elevations.

Foundation development moved between a frost-protected slab-on-grade concept and a traditional foundation. The final change was driven by the cost implications of applying the earlier approach to the property's existing profile.

## Constraints And Decisions

The primary constraint was change over time. The selected base design provided an efficient starting point, but the requested room arrangements and construction strategy continued to evolve. Each revision had to be carried across related plans, elevations, details, and notes.

Site economics also affected the technical direction. A slab-on-grade strategy became cost prohibitive when evaluated against the property profile, leading to a return to a traditional foundation. Municipal comments then required clearer documentation of structural, energy, ventilation, fire-separation, bracing, roofing, and floor-protection requirements.
