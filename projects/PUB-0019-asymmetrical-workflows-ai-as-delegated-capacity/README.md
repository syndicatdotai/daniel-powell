# Asymmetrical Workflows: AI as Delegated Capacity

## LinkedIn Summary

General contractors already understand the concept of asymmetrical work. You can be at one job while another crew is making progress somewhere else. Things can be happening and work can be getting done even when you are not personally on site.

I think that same understanding needs to shift toward AI.

AI can be working for you while you are also doing work: in the field, at the office, while you are sleeping, or while you are out with your family. Research can be gathered, documents can be organized, records can be checked, drafts can be prepared, and scheduled processes can continue without requiring you to sit in front of a computer for every step.

For a solo operator like myself, that is a meaningful change in capacity. The traditional limitation is that one person can only give full attention to one task at a time. With properly designed AI workflows, I can assign clearly defined work, provide the appropriate source material and rules, let the system work through the task, and review the result before it becomes final or goes anywhere external.

This is different from asking a chatbot a question and waiting for an immediate answer. It is closer to delegation. The task needs a clear scope, the right context, access to the correct tools, a defined output, validation, and approval boundaries. Some work can run on a schedule. Other work can be started from the field and reviewed later when I am back at the office.

The value is not staying busy around the clock or removing human judgment. The value is separating progress from my immediate attention. While I am handling construction, drafting, meetings, or family time, another part of the operation can still move forward.

General contractors already understand that progress can happen when they are not on site. AI extends that same operating principle into research, administration, documentation, software development, and planning.

The next productivity shift may not be working faster. It may be learning how to have more useful work happening at the same time.

The idea is familiar in construction: a general contractor can be meeting with a client, visiting one site, or working in the office while employees, subcontractors, suppliers, and other project participants continue advancing defined work elsewhere.

Daniel applies the same general principle to AI-assisted operations. A task can be scoped, supplied with the correct context and tools, assigned to Codex or a scheduled workflow, and returned for review. This allows research, administration, documentation, software work, and planning to move forward while Daniel is handling fieldwork, office responsibilities, meetings, or personal time.

The comparison is about delegated capacity, not replacing responsibility. The operator still defines the work, provides or approves the source information, establishes limits, reviews consequential results, and controls external actions.

His role includes setting the objective, selecting the authoritative information, specifying the expected output, establishing validation requirements, deciding what Codex may change, and retaining approval over consequential or external actions.

As a solo operator, Daniel is also the system designer and primary reviewer. Construction and drafting experience help him distinguish between work that can be delegated safely and work that still requires direct observation, professional judgment, customer communication, or hands-on execution.

## Contractor Analogy

General contractors already operate through distributed responsibility. Progress does not require the contractor to be physically present beside every person performing every task. A contractor defines scope, coordinates access and sequencing, communicates expectations, checks progress, resolves exceptions, and reviews completed work.

AI introduces another form of delegated capacity. It can work with digital information and software while the operator is occupied somewhere else. The useful comparison is not that an AI system is equivalent to a tradesperson. It is that both arrangements require clear assignments, access to the right information and tools, known boundaries, observable progress, and review of the finished result.

This framing makes the technology easier to evaluate. Instead of asking whether AI can replace an entire role, the practical question becomes: `Which defined parts of the operation can continue without requiring my immediate attention?`

## Scope Of Work

Daniel's asymmetrical workflow covers selected forms of digital work that can be defined, checked, and returned for review. These include:

- Researching a documented topic from supplied questions or source links.
- Organizing field notes, customer intake, project facts, and follow-up items.
- Preparing administrative records and draft business documents.
- Reviewing existing records for missing, conflicting, or unresolved information.
- Maintaining controlled queues for approved public content and recurring work.
- Preparing drawing, software, and documentation tasks within established workflows.
- Running scheduled research or publishing processes when their inputs have already been reviewed.
- Producing a result, exception report, or review packet for Daniel rather than silently treating the task as complete.

The scope does not include autonomous field judgment, unrestricted customer communication, unapproved commitments, or bypassing the review requirements attached to a workflow.

## Four Operating Modes

### Parallel Work

Daniel can perform construction, drafting, meetings, or office work while Codex handles a separate, clearly defined digital assignment. Both work streams move forward during the same period, even though only one requires Daniel's continuous attention.

### Remote Work

A plain-language instruction sent from the field can initiate supported administrative work on the connected primary system. This lets information enter the operating record closer to when it is received and allows work to begin before Daniel returns to the office.

### Scheduled Work

Some processes operate from reviewed queues or inputs at defined times. A nightly research workflow can process supplied source material, and the LinkedIn publishing system can release previously approved commentary on its schedule. Scheduled operation is limited to systems with defined inputs, controls, and failure handling.

### Review-Gated Work

Many assignments can be prepared without Daniel watching every intermediate step but cannot become final without his review. Draft documents, public records, uncertain classifications, and consequential changes remain held until the required approval is provided.

## Important Features

### Clear Delegation

Each useful assignment starts with a defined objective. Broad requests are broken into bounded tasks with known inputs, destinations, and completion criteria.

### Persistent Context

The workflow can use established project, knowledge, schedule, task, and document records rather than requiring Daniel to reconstruct the background every time work resumes.

### Tool Access

Codex can use the software, scripts, files, and connected services authorized for a particular workflow. Access remains limited by the task and does not imply unrestricted control of the business.

### Observable Progress

Queues, records, run logs, output files, and status fields make it possible to determine what was requested, what changed, whether the work succeeded, and what still needs attention.

### Validation And Stop Conditions

Supported workflows define checks for required information, expected outputs, conflicting records, or failed external actions. Uncertainty is returned for review rather than filled with invented information.

### Approval Boundaries

Preparation can happen asynchronously, but publication, external delivery, consequential record changes, and final business decisions remain subject to the authorization required by each workflow.

## Delegation Model

A typical assignment follows a controlled sequence:

1. Daniel identifies a task that can progress without continuous supervision.
2. He provides the objective, relevant source information, and any required constraints.
3. Codex resolves the instruction against the established workflow and authoritative records.
4. The system performs the supported research, organization, drafting, validation, or software operation.
5. Results and exceptions are written to a durable location with enough context for review.
6. Daniel reviews the work and resolves any questions that require judgment.
7. Final or external action occurs only when the workflow's approval requirements are satisfied.

This sequence allows attention and execution to happen at different times without losing accountability.

## Constraints And Decisions

Asymmetrical work depends on preparation. A vague assignment with incomplete context does not become reliable simply because AI can run for a long time. Useful delegation requires clear scope, stable source information, appropriate tool access, defined output, and a way to recognize failure.

Scheduled work also requires stronger boundaries than an interactive conversation. A system operating while Daniel is asleep cannot pause for casual clarification in the same way. It must either have sufficient reviewed inputs or stop cleanly and leave an exception for later review.

Not every task belongs in this model. Physical construction, field verification, customer commitments, ambiguous project decisions, and unusual edge conditions may require Daniel's direct participation. The goal is to move suitable work forward, not to force every responsibility into automation.

The system avoids measuring success by continuous activity. The intended result is useful progress and returned attention, including the ability to spend time with family without carrying every administrative step personally in the moment.

## Deliverables

Depending on the assignment, the asymmetrical workflow can produce:

- Structured research notes with source references and uncertainties.
- Organized project, customer, task, and schedule information.
- Draft estimates, invoices, contracts, and supporting records for review.
- Prepared public content held behind approval and publishing controls.
- Software changes, documentation, or test results within an authorized development task.
- Drawing or production outputs generated through a specialized application.
- Validation reports identifying successful steps, missing information, and exceptions.
- Durable queue and status records showing what is pending, completed, failed, or awaiting review.

## Outcome

The main outcome is increased operating capacity without requiring Daniel to be continuously present at the computer. Work can begin in the field, continue through a scheduled process, or progress alongside construction and office responsibilities. Results can be waiting for review instead of the entire task waiting for a future opening in Daniel's schedule.

For a solo operator, this changes the relationship between time and progress. Daniel still has one set of hands and remains responsible for judgment, but multiple defined work streams can advance during the same period.

The concept is already familiar in construction: progress can happen even when the general contractor is not personally on site. AI extends that principle into digital work, where delegation can support research, administration, documentation, software development, and planning.

The productivity gain is therefore not limited to performing one task faster. It comes from having more useful work moving forward at the same time while maintaining review, accountability, and control.
