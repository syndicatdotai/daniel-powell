# Finding the trust threshold for AI delegation

## LinkedIn Summary

One of the most useful changes in my relationship with AI has been learning how easy it is to offload trivial decisions and tasks.

There is still a threshold, usually the monetary stakes or the weightiness of a decision, that warrants excruciating analysis of the output, almost to the point where I could have just done the work myself.

Part of this journey is experimenting with AI and pushing it to its boundaries: seeing what it is actually capable of, and finding where my own internal thresholds sit.

The interesting overlap is between full trust and no trust. Some work can be delegated almost completely. Other work still requires me to inspect every assumption and keep final judgment for myself.

The goal isn't blind trust. It is learning where trust becomes a useful operating tool, and where it doesn't.

## Overview

This record describes a personal shift from treating AI as an occasional assistant to treating it as a place where some trivial decisions and tasks can be offloaded. The delegation is not uniform. As the monetary stakes or human weight of a decision increase, the amount of scrutiny applied to the output can increase until the review is so extensive that doing the work manually would almost have been simpler.

## Role

Daniel decides what can be delegated, defines the level of scrutiny, tests the output, and remains responsible for decisions that carry meaningful consequences.

AI provides draft work, recommendations, organization, and task execution within the boundary Daniel sets. The system does not determine the trust boundary on its own.

## Scope Of Work

The workflow covers:

- offloading routine or trivial decisions and tasks;
- testing AI on work with different levels of consequence;
- identifying where a task can be accepted with light review;
- applying deeper inspection when a decision has greater monetary stakes or weightiness; and
- recording the difference between useful delegation and work that would be faster or safer to do directly.

## Key Actions

1. Give AI a bounded task or decision rather than treating every request as equally consequential.
2. Observe what the system can complete without extensive intervention.
3. Compare the output against the actual stakes and responsibility attached to the decision.
4. Increase scrutiny when the consequences of an error are meaningful.
5. Notice when review becomes so exhaustive that manual completion would have been nearly as efficient.
6. Use the result of each experiment to recalibrate the personal boundary between trust and distrust.

## Important Features

### Graduated delegation

Trust is not a single yes-or-no setting. It changes with the task, the available context, and the consequences of being wrong.

### A visible review threshold

The threshold is often felt through the amount of analysis required. Some outputs can move forward with a quick check; others invite an almost excruciating examination of assumptions, reasoning, and details.

### Boundary testing

Pushing AI to its boundaries is part of learning the system. The experiments reveal not only what the model can do, but also where Daniel's own internal standards require more control.

### Responsibility remains human

Delegating a task does not transfer responsibility for the decision. The useful question is how much work can be handed off while preserving appropriate judgment.

## Constraints And Decisions

The post intentionally avoids specific financial examples, amounts, private decisions, clients, and projects. It describes a personal calibration process rather than a universal formula for deciding when AI can be trusted.

Greater scrutiny is appropriate when the decision has meaningful financial or human consequences, when source information is incomplete, or when the output will be relied on externally. In those cases, the review burden may approach the effort of producing the work directly.

## Deliverables

This workflow can produce:

- routine decisions and administrative tasks completed with limited intervention;
- draft outputs for review;
- a clearer sense of which tasks are suitable for delegation;
- a record of where review effort becomes disproportionate; and
- a personal trust boundary that can be adjusted as experience accumulates.

## Outcome

The outcome is better calibration. Daniel is learning where AI can carry trivial work, where it can prepare useful output, and where the decision still warrants close personal inspection. The value is not blind trust; it is knowing when trust is a useful operating tool and when it is not.

## Tools And Methods

- **AI assistants and agents:** delegated task execution, organization, and draft generation;
- **Natural-language instructions:** defining the task and the expected output;
- **Boundary experiments:** testing tasks with different levels of consequence;
- **Graduated review:** adjusting scrutiny to the monetary stakes or weightiness of a decision; and
- **Human judgment:** accepting, revising, rejecting, or taking over the work when the trust threshold is not met.

## Evidence And Limitations

This record is grounded primarily in Daniel's direct description of his current AI experimentation and trust calibration. Related private workflow records support the broader pattern of delegated capacity, routine administration, controlled tool use, and human review.

The public record does not claim a measured accuracy result, a specific model capability, or a universal threshold for safe delegation. It does not identify a private decision or financial amount. The boundary between full trust and no trust remains contextual and must be evaluated for each task.
