# Southern Connecticut Shopping Center Life-Safety Coordination

## LinkedIn Summary

Looking back at 2020, one project that stands out from my time with Infinity Group involved life-safety upgrades at a shopping center in southern Connecticut.

The immediate work centered on a tenant space where the fire-rated separation between the storefront and common corridor needed to be brought into compliance. What appeared straightforward required coordinated work above the ceiling, where wall continuity, penetrations, framing, drywall, sprinkler coverage, fire alarm devices, and smoke detection had to function as one system.

My role was to lead the project from field investigation through execution. That included documenting existing conditions, developing the scope, coordinating subcontractor buyout, managing light-gauge framing and drywall work, and coordinating the fire suppression and alarm trades. It also required schedule management, communication between teams, field verification, and confirming the completed work met the intended life-safety requirements.

Alongside the active construction, I investigated above-ceiling conditions across roughly fifteen additional tenant spaces. Each space required careful review of the rated assemblies and the systems passing through or serving them. That investigation created a clearer record of existing conditions and gave Infinity Group the information needed to plan the remaining work responsibly.

Completing the initial space established a practical baseline for the larger effort. It showed how field investigation, clear scopes, and coordinated trade execution could turn concealed and sometimes fragmented conditions into an organized project plan.

This project remains a good example of what I valued about working with Infinity Group: collaboration between project leadership, trade partners, and field teams, with a strong focus on understanding the conditions before committing to the solution.

Much of the important information was concealed above finished ceilings. ## Trade Coordination

Life-safety work frequently crosses several ownership boundaries. Framing and drywall establish the physical assembly, while sprinkler coverage, alarm devices, and detection components must remain coordinated with that construction. A change in one area can affect access, sequencing, or verification in another.

Daniel's role was to keep those scopes connected. This included developing clear trade responsibilities, coordinating field access and execution, and confirming that the initial completed space reflected the intended combined solution rather than a collection of disconnected trade tasks.
