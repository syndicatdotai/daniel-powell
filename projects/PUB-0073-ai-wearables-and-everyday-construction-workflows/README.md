# AI Wearables and Everyday Construction Workflows

## LinkedIn Summary

I'm seeing more construction companies taking an interest in AI wearables for work in the field.

Smart glasses like the Even Realities G2 and wearable AI note takers like the Plaud NotePin S are examples of the hardware I'm paying attention to. Being able to capture a conversation, keep track of observations, or refer to information without constantly reaching for a phone fits the way construction work actually happens.

My opinion is that construction software, AI workflows, and wearable hardware are getting close to being ready for prime time together. The hardware looks considerably more practical than earlier generations, and the software has more ways to work with the information being captured.

I'm interested in how those pieces can connect to the job records and documentation we're already maintaining.

## Overview

I'm noticing more interest in AI wearables among construction companies. My view is that improvements in hardware and AI software are making the combination more practical for everyday field work.

## Role

I'm following these developments from the perspective of someone already using voice and AI to capture information and maintain construction records.

## Scope Of Work

The devices I'm watching include display glasses and wearable audio recorders. I'm interested in capturing observations, referring to information, and getting field context into job documentation.

## Key Actions

Even Realities calls its display glasses Even G2. Its Conversate feature provides conversation support and produces transcripts and summaries through the companion app. [Even G2 product information](https://www.evenrealities.com/en-US/smart-glasses)

Plaud NotePin S is a wearable AI note taker that captures conversations and turns recordings into transcripts, summaries, and shareable outputs through Plaud's ecosystem. [Plaud NotePin S overview](https://support.plaud.ai/hc/en-us/articles/53770498052633-What-is-Plaud-NotePin-S)

## Important Features

These products put information capture or access on something a person can wear. For construction work, I'm interested in how that could fit into site visits, observations, and documentation throughout the day.

Construction already has examples of wearable capture connected to software. OpenSpace documents Balfour Beatty's use of hardhat mounted cameras for site documentation and describes AI tools supporting the team's work. [OpenSpace's Balfour Beatty case study](https://www.openspace.ai/resources/case-studies/balfour-beatty-openspace-case-study/)

## Constraints And Decisions

My assessment of readiness is an opinion about the direction of the hardware and software. A useful workflow still needs to connect captured information to the correct job records and documents.
