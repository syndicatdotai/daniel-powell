# Designing AI workflows for construction work in the field

## LinkedIn Summary

I think a lot of AI consulting misses how construction actually operates in the field.

If you spend your day in front of a computer, it's easy to build a workflow that assumes everyone else can do the same. On a job site, screen time is limited. Stopping to type, organize files, or work through software takes time away from the job. Otherwise, the admin piles up for the evening.

For me, the big unlock has been short voice snippets and plain speech. I can dictate what happened, explain what needs to be done, and have the AI work on the admin behind it. Update the job notes, organize information, prepare a document, or start some research.

I can keep the field work moving while giving those instructions throughout the day. A construction workflow needs to account for that limited access to a screen.

## Overview

My criticism of a lot of AI consulting is that it starts with the assumptions of a desk job. A workflow can look useful on a computer and still be awkward to use during a day of construction work.

In the field, access to a screen is limited. Administration competes with productive time on the job or gets pushed into the evening. That constraint shapes how I use AI.

## Role

I bring the context from the work in the field and dictate what needs to happen. The AI uses those instructions to carry out supporting administrative tasks.

My perspective comes from using these workflows alongside construction work, where sitting down at a computer is often a separate activity from getting the job done.

## Scope Of Work

The workflow covers capturing job information, updating notes, organizing incoming context, preparing documents, and starting research through spoken instructions.

The focus is making those tasks accessible during the working day, without requiring a long session at a screen for every request.

## Key Actions

I use short voice snippets to describe what happened or explain a task. I can give the AI the relevant project context and tell it what to do with that information in ordinary language.

Those instructions can set administrative work in motion while I continue with the field work. Depending on the task, that may mean adding commentary to the job record, organizing information, or preparing material I need next.

## Important Features

Voice input fits the way information arrives on a job. A conversation, an observation, or a decision can be described in plain speech and directed into the appropriate workflow.

I don't have to turn each piece of information into a separate typing and filing session before the administrative work can begin.

## Constraints And Decisions

Limited screen access is a practical design requirement. A workflow needs to account for where the person is working and how much attention they can give to the interface.

For my use, that means making spoken instructions a normal way to start work and provide context.

## Deliverables

Job notes, organized project information, document preparation, and research initiated through voice instructions from the field.

## Outcome

The significant change for me is being able to move administrative work forward throughout the day. I can give instructions as the need arises, instead of having every task depend on the next opportunity to sit at the computer.

## Tools And Methods

AI assistance, short voice dictation, plain language instructions, project context, and administrative workflows connected to the field work.

## Evidence And Limitations

This is my perspective on workflow design and my own experience using AI in construction. The criticism concerns approaches that assume continual screen access; it is not a survey of individual consultants. No client information or private project details are included.
